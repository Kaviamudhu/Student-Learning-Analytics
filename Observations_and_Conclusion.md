## Task-wise findings
1. **Study hours and marks:** The data shows a strong positive linear association (r = 0.981). In this sample, students who study more hours generally tend to obtain higher marks. Correlation does not prove that study time alone causes higher marks.
2. **Assignments:** Students completing more assignments generally tend to score higher. The correlation between assignments completed and marks is 0.993. This is an association, not proof of causation.
3. **Students needing support:** S205, S212 scored below 50 and are classified as Needs Improvement. They should receive individual academic support. Students scoring exactly 50 are classified as Good under the given rule.
4. **Best visualization:** A scatter plot is the most suitable chart for examining the relationship between two numerical variables (study hours and exam marks). A trendline can be added in Excel for further analysis.

## Recommendations
- Encourage students to follow a consistent daily study timetable.
- Break large topics into smaller learning goals and use active-recall and spaced-revision techniques.
- Monitor assignment completion and provide timely feedback.
- Track attendance and contact students when attendance begins to decline.
- Arrange mentoring, remedial classes and doubt-clearing sessions for students requiring support.
- Review progress through periodic assessments rather than relying only on final examination marks.

## Conclusion
The sample indicates that study hours, assignment completion and attendance are positively associated with examination performance. Students with lower marks can benefit from early intervention, structured study plans and regular faculty guidance. The dataset contains only 12 students, so the findings should be treated as preliminary and should not be generalized to the entire college without a larger, more representative dataset.
